A general education in mathematics - the middle way


Middle way mathematics (MWM) is a hobby level project to construct a curriculum and curricular content for a general education in mathematics. The curriculum targets all students; it is designed to provide an overview of the nature of modern mathematics. The curriculum is not designed to prepare students for further study in mathematics other than providing that same overview; STEM oriented students would require additional courses to prepare for further mathematical study. 

Mathematics is assumed to be a formal science. This assumption provides the page's perspective on the ontological status of mathematics: it lives in a world of supposition, a bridge that connects our intuition with our experience, a world where we ask "what if?", and see where it leads, and, eventually, how well it can model our observations. However, this ontological stance does not preclude the possibility that the formalism models something. In fact, this is assumed by the page. It allows middle way mathematics to be described as an effective model for a pedagogic description of modern mathematics. From the beginning, we acknowledge there is a world of mathematics out there, and MWM presents a model of a small, albeit essential, slice of that world. As the diagram illustrates, MWM is focused on the mathematics supporting mathematical physics. The fundamental design parameters for a general curriculum in mathematics with this focus include

  • what physical theories can be modeled
  • and to what level of depth of understanding is the mathematics modeling the theory understood.

The curricular goal of MWM is not providing the approximately 25% of STEM oriented students the prerequisite knowledge for further study; instead, the curricular goal of MWM is to provide motivated students with a foundation for a life long consumption of popular science that can include an appreciation of the formal, as well as conceptual, notions at play when a physical theory is described.

The hallmark of any formal science is that it is expressed formally, although the formal statements will be embedded in NL explanations. The page assumes an unadorned single-sorted FOL and ZF set theory as it's formal foundation. That is, the page adopts about the simplest formal foundation possible. But it important to note that the page makes no claim that this formalism has the expressive power to model all of mathematics. 

MWM is an effective model for a pedagogic description of modern mathematics. An effective model asserts as axioms established mathematical facts. In the case of MWM, the cornerstone axiom is that 

  • (No(ε0), +, ·) is a totally ordered algebraic field

where

  • the carrier set No(ε0) is the set of Conway number tree nodes whose birthday is less than the ordinal ε0
  • and where the total order and field level arithmetic operations are recursively defined with respect to birthday.

The task then is two-fold

  1. construct No(ε0) := (No(ε0), +, ·)  - where we often refer to the field by it's carrier denotation
  2. and then assert the axiom that this construction is a totally ordered field.

An important property of No(ε0) is that it is non-Archimedean. This eliminates standard analysis as a path forward. Instead, No(ε0) is the framework for a nonstandard analysis. It is the ambient field for all of arithmetic.

Construction of the ordinals Ord is an important step in the construction of No(ε0), as ordinals index (the transfinite) growth of number trees. The construction assumes

  • the natural numbers are isomorphic to the finite ordinals
  • ω denotes the supremum of the finite ordinals
  • and ω+1 < ε0.
In general, No(α), where α is an ordinal, denotes all number tree nodes whose birthday is less than α; we use this to denote two sets

  • No(ω) - the set of all finite ordinals, and
  • No(ω+1) - {the set of all finite ordinals} union {the set of all number tree nodes whose birthday is ω}.

We consider the commutative ring (No(ω), +, ·) where the operations are inherited from No(ε0).  After, demonstrating the isomorphism between (No(ω), +, ·) and (𝔻, +, ·) where the elements of 𝔻 are dyadic rationals and operations on 𝔻 are those of dyadic arithmetic, 𝔻 becomes the arena of all numeric calculation. Thus numeric calculation occurs within the context of a principal ideal domain rather than a field.

We do not consider any algebraic structure for which No(ω+1) is the carrier set. Instead we only use No(ω+1) as a source for numbers that can be used in algebraic expressions, as part of a syntactic vocabulary. But we note that these numbers also exist in No(ε0), and so are elements of an algebraic field. We also note that x ∈ ℝ ⇒ x ∈ No(ω+1); however, there is no algebraic structure associated with No(ω+1) with which to compare to the Archimedean field (ℝ, +, ·). And we finally note that No(ω+1) includes elements not in ℝ. In particular No(ω+1) includes non-finite and infinitesimal numbers.

MWM defines a function that takes as input

  • an algebraic expression that may include elements of No(ω+1)
  • and a natural number denoting a precision p
and will generate an algebraic expression that replaces each denoted No(ω+1) element with their tree address truncated to the first p directions. As part of this process the 'standard' standard part behavior of nonstandard analysis is mimicked to handle non-finite and infinitesimal numbers.
