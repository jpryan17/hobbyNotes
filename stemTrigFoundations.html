Trigonometry on the Conway Number Tree & ℂ_ω

A Constructive STEM Perspective — From Discrete Dyadic Branching to Complex Continuum Rotation

The connection between John Conway’s number tree and trigonometry is both mathematically profound and computationally practical. When viewed through the lens of binary branching trees and the nonstandard complex grid ℂ_ω = ℝ_ω ⊗ ℝ_ω, trigonometric identities, hardware algorithms, and complex analysis cease to be arbitrary formulas and reveal their natural inductive geometry.

Core Conceptual Shift: Classical trigonometry builds on transcendental continuous functions and limits. The Conway approach reveals that circular rotation is the natural continuum shadow of simple binary choices (dyadic bisections) and hyperfinite perpendicular steps.

Part 1 The Real Conway Tree: Discrete Bisections & Algorithms

1. Angle Bisection & Nested Radicals (Viète’s Formula)

At finite Conway birthdays n, the 2-successor tree generates the dense set of dyadic rationals 𝔻 = { m / 2ⁿ }. Mapping the normalized interval [0, 1) to the circular domain [0, 2π) reveals that descending the tree corresponds precisely to successive angle bisection:

  • Day 0: Root 0 (Angle 0)
  • Day 1: Semi-circle poles (Angles 0, π)
  • Day 2: Quadrant axis markers (Angles π/2, 3π/2)
  • Day 3: The classical 45° diagonals (Angles π/4, 3π/4, 5π/4, 7π/4)
  • Day n: Granular binary angles θ_n = 2π / 2ⁿ

The geometric rule for stepping down along the positive branch is given by the half-angle cosine identity:

cos(θ / 2) = √[ (1 + cos θ) / 2 ]

Starting from cos(π/2) = 0 and stepping recursively down the branches yields nested square roots of 2:

cos(π/4) = √2 / 2
cos(π/8) = √[ 2 + √2 ] / 2
cos(π/16) = √[ 2 + √(2 + √2) ] / 2
cos(π/2ⁿ⁺¹) = √[ 2 + √(2 + ... + √2) ] / 2   (n nested radicals)

Multiplying these branch cosines as we descend toward transfinite Day ω reproduces François Viète’s famous 1593 infinite product for π:

2 / π = (√2 / 2) · (√[2 + √2] / 2) · (√[2 + √(2 + √2)] / 2) · ...

On the Conway tree, this product is not an ad-hoc analytic trick: it is the exact cumulative projection of radial coordinates as you zoom in toward the origin.

2. The CORDIC Algorithm: How Hardware Actually Computes Trig

Microprocessors, GPS receivers, and pocket calculators rarely compute sines and cosines with Taylor series, because multiplications and divisions consume excessive clock cycles and accumulate floating-point rounding errors.

Instead, modern hardware implements the CORDIC algorithm (Coordinate Rotation Digital Computer), which is literally a path traversal along a Conway sign sequence:

  1. Any input angle θ is expressed as a sum of precomputed dyadic elementary angles:
    θ ≈ ∑[k=0 to n] d_k · arctan(2⁻ᵏ),   where d_k ∈ { -1, +1 }
  2. At each clock cycle k, the hardware evaluates a single binary question: Am I clockwise or counterclockwise from the target?
    • If counterclockwise (d_k = +1), rotate forwards.
    • If clockwise (d_k = -1), rotate backwards.
  3. Because the angular step factor is 2⁻ᵏ, vector updates require zero multiplications—only bit-shifts (dividing by 2) and additions:
    x_(k+1) = x_k - d_k · y_k · 2⁻ᵏ
    y_(k+1) = y_k + d_k · x_k · 2⁻ᵏ

Executing trigonometry in physical silicon is mathematically isomorphic to walking a path of Left and Right choices down a Conway tree of dyadic angles.

3. Walsh Functions: The "Square-Wave Trig" of the Binary Tree

In continuous space, smooth sines and cosines form an orthogonal basis for classical Fourier analysis. On a discrete binary tree, the exact counterparts to sinusoidal waves are Walsh-Hadamard functions.

  • Rather than undulating continuously, Walsh waves are square pulses taking values strictly in { -1, +1 }.
  • They are generated directly from the bit parity along Conway tree addresses (nim-addition / XOR operations).
  • This makes Walsh-Hadamard transforms the native trigonometric language of digital signal processing, CDMA telecommunications, and quantum gate operations.

Part 2 The Complex Continuum ℂ_ω: From Algebra to Physical Reality

When we extend the Conway construction to transfinite Day ω and cross two real axes into the 2D complex plane ℂ_ω = ℝ_ω ⊗ ℝ_ω, trigonometry becomes the fundamental engine of geometry and physical conservation laws.

1. From Binary Tree to the 4-Successor Quadtree

On ℝ_ω, Conway’s tree branches in two directions: { -1, +1 }. In the complex domain ℂ_ω = { x + i·y | x, y ∈ ℝ_ω, i² = -1 }, the tree branches in four directions:

{ +1,   -1,   +i,   -i }

These are the 4th roots of unity, identifying the four cardinal directions:

e^(i · 0) = +1,    e^(i · π/2) = +i,    e^(i · π) = -1,    e^(i · 3π/2) = -i

Descending the complex Conway tree tiles the 2D plane with self-similar square cells of infinitesimal grid width dz = dx + i·dy, where dx = dy = 1/ω.

2. The Unit Circle Becomes an Exact Hyperfinite ω-gon

In classical analysis, the unit circle is an abstract continuous manifold whose perimeter requires transcendental limits. On ℂ_ω, the unit circle is an exact regular ω-sided polygon:

  • Number of vertices: Transfinite integer ω.
  • Infinitesimal step angle: dθ = 2π / ω = 2π · dx.
  • Vertex coordinates:
    z_k = e^(i · 2πk / ω) = cos(2πk / ω) + i · sin(2πk / ω)
  • Cosine is the horizontal projection onto the real tree ℝ_ω.
  • Sine is the vertical projection onto the imaginary tree i · ℝ_ω.

The circumference is evaluated as an elementary discrete sum without limits:

Perimeter = ∑[k=1 to ω] |z_k - z_(k-1)| = ω · |e^(i · 2π/ω) - 1| ≈ ω · (2π / ω) = 2π

Taking the standard part st(·) yields exactly 2π, with microscopic polygon chord errors vanishing as second-order infinitesimal dust O(1/ω²).

3. Euler’s Formula as Pure Perpendicular Stepping

Multiplying by i rotates any vector on ℂ_ω counterclockwise by exactly 90°. To trace circular rotation:

  1. At point z_k, take an infinitesimal step perpendicular to the radius:
    z_(k+1) = z_k + i · (θ / ω) · z_k = z_k · [ 1 + i · (θ / ω) ]
  2. Evaluate the change in radial length:
    |1 + i · (θ / ω)|² = 1 + (θ / ω)² = 1 + θ² · dx²
    The radial elongation is purely second-order dust O(dx²), which has zero standard part.
  3. Compounding ω perpendicular steps produces the circular trajectory:
    z_ω = [ 1 + i · (θ / ω) ]^ω ≡ e^(i·θ) = cos θ + i · sin θ

Smooth circular rotation (cos θ, sin θ) is simply the macroscopic shadow of marching perpendicular steps along the square grid cells of ℂ_ω!

4. Cauchy-Riemann Equations: The "No-Shear" Conformal Symmetry

A mapping f(z) = u(x, y) + i · v(x, y) on ℂ_ω is holomorphic if and only if it preserves the shape of the underlying Conway quadtree cells. The Cauchy-Riemann equations:

∂u/∂x = ∂v/∂y,      ∂u/∂y = -∂v/∂x

are the exact algebraic condition ensuring that every infinitesimal square cell maps to another square cell (scaled and rotated by e^(i·θ), with zero angle shear). Trigonometry is the intrinsic geometry of conformal invariance.

5. Why 2π i Appears in Contour Integrals

The famous factor 2π i in Cauchy’s Residue Theorem:

∮ (1 / z) dz = 2π i

is immediately transparent on the ℂ_ω polygon. Around the unit circle, z = e^(i·θ) and dz = i · e^(i·θ) dθ. Therefore:

dz / z = [ i · e^(i·θ) dθ ] / e^(i·θ) = i · dθ

∑[k=1 to ω] (dz / z) = ∑[k=1 to ω] i · (2π / ω) = i · 2π = 2π i

The residue factor 2π i is literally the total winding angle (2π) multiplied by the imaginary 90° rotation operator (i).

6. Quantum Phase: Why Nature Uses ℂ_ω Instead of ℝ_ω

In Quantum Logic, this geometric structure connects directly to physical reality:

  • Classical probabilities are non-negative real numbers in [0, 1]. They can only add constructively (p₁ + p₂).
  • Quantum states are complex amplitudes ψ = r · e^(i·θ) ∈ ℂ_ω carrying a trigonometric phase.
  • Because phases can point in opposite directions on ℂ_ω, two non-zero physical paths can destructively cancel:
    e^(i · 0) + e^(i · π) = 1 + (-1) = 0
  • The Pythagorean identity on ℂ_ω:
    cos²θ + sin²θ = 1
    is the fundamental law that guarantees unitarity and the conservation of total probability across all quantum state transformations.
