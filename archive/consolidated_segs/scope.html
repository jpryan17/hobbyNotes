In our hyperfinite, quantum-Bayesian context, the Fourier Transform loses all of its intimidating continuous measure-theoretic clutter ($\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}$, Lebesgue spaces, delta distributions) and reveals its true, elegant nature:

The Fourier Transform is simply a Unitary Change of Basis (a 90° hypercomplex rotation of coordinate axes) in our hyperfinite state space $\mathbb{C}^\omega$.

1. From Continuous Integrals to Hyperfinite Matrix Multiplication
In standard calculus textbooks, the Fourier Transform is defined as a continuous integral over infinite space.

On our transfinite grid $\mathbb{C}_\omega$ (where space is partitioned into $\omega$ hyperfinite cells of width $dx = 1/\omega$), a signal or quantum wavefunction $\psi(x)$ is simply a vector $|\psi\rangle \in \mathbb{C}^\omega$.

The Fourier Transform is just multiplying that spatial vector by an $\omega \times \omega$ unitary matrix of complex phase rotations:

$$\langle k_m \mid \psi \rangle \quad = \quad \frac{1}{\sqrt{\omega}} \sum_{n=1}^{\omega} \psi(x_n) \cdot e^{-i , k_m x_n}$$

2. The Three Core Pillars of Fourier in Our Context
Position Basis vs. Frequency Basis:

Spatial Position Basis ${ |x_n\rangle \rbrace$: Asks "How much probability amplitude is located at spatial cell $x_n$?"
Frequency Basis ${ |k_m\rangle \rbrace$: Asks "How much of the signal is rotating at spatial frequency $k_m$?"
The Fourier Matrix $F$ simply rotates our coordinate axes from spatial cells to pure phase modes $e^{i k x}$.
Probability Preservation ($U^\dagger U = I$):

As Jack and Jill established in Vectors Lectures 2 & 3, unitary matrices are probability-preserving rotations ($U^\dagger U = I$).
Plancherel / Parseval Theorem is not a complicated integral theorem here; it is simply vector length conservation: $$\langle \psi \mid \psi \rangle_{\text{space}} \quad = \quad \langle \psi \mid F^\dagger F \mid \psi \rangle \quad = \quad \langle \psi \mid \psi \rangle_{\text{frequency}}$$ Total probability in spatial cells equals total probability across frequency modes.
The Quantum Uncertainty Principle as Vector Geometry:

A state tightly localized at a single spatial cell ($|x_0\rangle$) projects equally onto every frequency mode $e^{-i k x_0}$ (a broad spectrum).
A state with a single pure frequency mode ($|k_0\rangle$) spreads uniformly across all spatial cells.
Heisenberg Uncertainty ($\Delta x \cdot \Delta p \ge \hbar/2$) becomes a direct visual property of vector projections on hyperfinite grids!
3. How Jane & Jack Would Summarize It
"Don't be intimidated by continuous Fourier integrals! The Fourier Transform is nothing more than taking a high-dimensional vector in $\mathbb{C}^\omega$ and looking at it through a rotated pair of glasses.

In spatial coordinates, we describe the vector cell-by-cell. In Fourier coordinates, we describe the exact same vector in terms of pure phase rotations $e^{i k x}$. Because the rotation matrix is unitary ($F^\dagger F = I$), no information is destroyed—it is just two different ways of looking at the exact same physical reality."
