Lecture 3: Quantum Measurement as Vector Projection
The Geometric Upgrade of the Bayesian Filter

1. From Subset Slicing to Vector Projection

In classical Bayesian inference, observing data E acts as a cookie-cutter filter: it slices out the subset of points on the transect that are incompatible with E and stretches the surviving segment back to 100%.

In quantum mechanics, states are directional vectors (arrows) in complex space ℋ_ω. Measuring a quantum state in direction E acts as a geometric projector: it drops a perpendicular from the state vector onto the measurement axis and rescales the surviving arrow back to unit length!

Classical Bayes: Slicing Subsets on ℝ_ω ⟺ Quantum Bayes: Projecting Vectors on ℂ_ω

2. The Geometric Probability Law (The Dot Product)

Suppose a quantum system is prepared in state vector |v⟩ (a unit arrow). If we measure whether the system is in state |u⟩ (a detector pointing in direction |u⟩), what is the probability of detection?

The Geometric Rule: The probability is simply the squared cosine of the angle θ between the two vectors:

P(Detection)=∣⟨u∣v⟩∣2=cos2(θ)P(\text{Detection}) = |\langle u | v \rangle|^2 = \cos^2(\theta)

  • Parallel / Aligned (θ = 0°): cos²(0°) = 1.00 ⇒ 100% Certainty.
  • Perpendicular / Orthogonal (θ = 90°): cos²(90°) = 0.00 ⇒ 0% Probability (Impossible).
  • Diagonal (θ = 45°): cos²(45°) = (1/√2)² = 0.50 ⇒ 50% Probability (Fifty-Fifty Split).

3. Solving the 3-Polarizer Mystery with Vector Geometry

Now we can solve the 3-polarizer puzzle from Lecture 1 using simple 2D geometry:

  1. Initial State: Light passes Filter A (Horizontal 0°). State vector is pointing along the X-axis:
    |v_0⟩ = (1, 0)^T (Horizontal 0°)

  2. Inserting Filter C (at 45°): The angle between 0° and 45° is θ = 45°. The probability of passing is cos²(45°) = 50%. The surviving light vector drops a perpendicular onto the 45° line, rotating into:
    |v_1⟩ = (1/√2, 1/√2)^T (Diagonal 45°)

  3. Reaching Filter B (Vertical 90°): The angle between 45° and 90° is again θ = 45°! The probability of passing is another cos²(45°) = 50%.
Total Light Output = 50% × 50% = 25%!

The diagonal filter did not "open a secret door"—it geometrically projected the vector into a new direction that had a non-zero overlap with the vertical detector!

4. The Quantum Bayesian Filter (Lüders Projection Rule)

Just as Bayes' rule updates a classical probability distribution upon learning evidence, the Lüders Projection Rule updates a quantum state upon measurement:

Classical Bayesian Update (ℝ_ω) Quantum Bayesian Update (ℂ_ω)
Prior State: Probability vector P(s) on Ω Prior State: Directional state vector |ψ⟩ in ℋ_ω
Evidence Filter: Slice subset E ⊂ Ω Measurement Filter: Projection operator P_E = |u⟩⟨u|
Normalization Factor (Evidence):
P(E) = ∑_{s ∈ E} P(s) 		Normalization Factor (Evidence):
P(E) = || P_E |ψ⟩ ||² = |⟨u | ψ⟩|²
Posterior State:
P(s | E) = (P(s) · 𝕀_E(s)) / P(E) 		Posterior State:
|ψ'⟩ = (P_E |ψ⟩) / √P(E)
