1. Canonical Conjugate Pairs in Physics

In classical Hamiltonian mechanics and quantum mechanics, two physical variables (q, p) are defined as canonically conjugate if they satisfy Dirac's fundamental canonical commutation relation:

[x̂, p̂]  =  x̂ · p̂ − p̂ · x̂  =  i · ℏ · I

Whenever two physical quantities satisfy this commutation relation, one is the Fourier Transform dual of the other:

2. Why Fourier Transform = Canonical Conjugacy

Examine the exponent of the Fourier Transform kernel:

K(x, p)  =  e−(i / ℏ) · p · x

Notice what sits inside the exponent: the product of the two canonically conjugate variables (p · x) divided by Quantum Action ℏ!

  • The Fourier Transform is the unique unitary transformation that diagonalizes the momentum differential operator: p̂ = −iℏ · (d / dx).
  • In position space, x̂ acts as simple multiplication by x, while p̂ acts as a derivative (d / dx).
  • When you Fourier-transform to momentum space, the roles swap: p̂ acts as simple multiplication by p, while x̂ acts as a derivative iℏ · (d / dp)!
