Hello everyone,

I'd like to share an open-source project exploring how to bring machine-checked formal reasoning into **mainstream General Education (Gen Ed)** mathematics without placing heavy DevOps or cloud burdens on instructors: **Middle Way Mathematics** (https://www.middlewaymath.app | repo: https://github.com/jpryan17/hobbyNotes).

The project proposes two complementary solutions:

1. **The Civic Utility Model (Educational Resource Hubs):**
   Hosting stateless Lean 4 kernel workers and Maxima CAS evaluation as open public compute APIs, while delivering zero-login, zero-dependency static pages to students with zero server crashes or per-seat licensing fees.
2. **The Gen Ed Curriculum & The Inductive Continuum:**
   An intuitive, constructive bridge from discrete computation to continuous calculus. Our whitepaper outlines reducing ~40 operational scaffolds down to **Three Inductive Seeds** (discrete tree generation, an infinitesimal monad via `st(·)`, and invariant duality) designed for straightforward Lean 4 formalization without heavy topological machinery.

The 52-segment curriculum is live to browse with embedded Lean 4 proof stencils. If you explore any chapter or proposal, there is an in-situ **`[💬 Feedback]`** button at the top right of each page that logs notes directly with segment context for my assistant and me to review.

We'd love your thoughts on the hub architecture, the 3-seed inductive core, or pedagogical strategies for Gen Ed formal logic!
