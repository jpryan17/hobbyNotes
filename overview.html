A General Education in Mathematics: The Middle Way

Middle Way Mathematics (MWM) is a curriculum and foundational framework for a general education in mathematics. The curriculum targets all motivated students, providing a clear overview of the nature of modern mathematical thought. It is not designed to prepare students for technical STEM tracks; rather, its goal is to provide a conceptual and formal literacy that enables lifelong, thoughtful engagement with formal descriptions - offered in more sophisticated popular science literature and video lectures - of modern physical theories.

1. Mathematics as a Formal Science

Mathematics is treated as a formal science whose home is a world of supposition. It serves as a bridge linking human intuition with empirical experience—an arena where we ask "What if?", trace deductive consequences, and evaluate how effectively the resulting structures model physical observations, as illustrated.

MWM does not claim to model all of mathematics. It focuses specifically on the mathematics supporting mathematical physics, balancing two design parameters:

  1. What physical theories can be modeled?
  2. At what level of depth can the modeling mathematics be understood?
MWM expresses its concepts formally, embedded in natural language explanations, using single-sorted first-order logic and ZF set theory as its foundational baseline.
📐 Mathematical Foundations: Fundamental Assumptions and Lean Verification

The sections below detail the formal, non-Archimedean axiomatic bedrock of Middle Way Mathematics for mathematicians, curriculum architects, and system evaluators.

2. The Cornerstone Axiom, Ordinals, and Number Trees

MWM operates as an effective model: rather than building foundational set-theoretic proofs from scratch, it asserts established mathematical structures as governing axioms. The cornerstone axiom of MWM is:

(No(ε₀), +, ·) is a totally ordered algebraic field

where:

  • The carrier set No(ε₀) consists of nodes on the 2-successor number tree whose tree depth (birthday) is strictly less than the ordinal ε₀.
  • The total order (≤) and the arithmetic operations (+, ·) are defined recursively with respect to the node's stage of creation, i.e. it's birthday.

Because No(ε₀) is non-Archimedean, standard ε–δ analysis is set aside in favor of a constructive nonstandard analysis. No(ε₀) serves as the ambient field for all arithmetic and continuous modeling.

📜 Historical Development: From Conway to Gonshor to van den Dries & Ehrlich (1976–2001)

The recognition of No(ε₀) as the natural ambient field for arithmetic represents the culmination of a quarter-century of research in transfinite algebra and model theory:

1976 — John H. Conway (On Numbers and Games): Conway introduced the surreal numbers No and demonstrated that the full collection forms a proper-class real-closed field containing both the real numbers and the transfinite ordinals. Conway formulated numbers game-theoretically as pairs of sets x = { L | R } where every element of L is strictly less than every element of R. However, because No as a whole is a proper class rather than a set, standard mathematical foundations and computational frameworks require well-founded, set-sized subfields.
1986 — Harry Gonshor (An Introduction to the Theory of Surreal Numbers): Gonshor achieved a key homogenization by reformulating Conway's surreal numbers into standard 2-successor tree terminology—defining numbers as transfinite sign-expansions (sequences of + and - branches along a binary tree). This structural homogenization replaced pair-of-sets cuts with explicit tree paths and birthdays, establishing foundational algebraic properties for initial segments while leaving open the question of which ordinal cutoffs yield closed subfields.
2001 — Lou van den Dries & Philip Ehrlich ("Fields of surreal numbers and exponentiation", Fund. Math. 167): Working within this 2-successor sign-expansion framework, logicians Lou van den Dries and Philip Ehrlich published the definitive classification theorem: The birthday-truncated collection No(α) forms an elementary subfield of No if and only if α is an ε-number (an ordinal fixed point satisfying ω^α = α). Because Cantor's ordinal ε₀ = sup { ω, ω^ω, ω^(ω^ω), ... } is the very first non-zero ε-number, No(ε₀) is proven to be the unique minimal set-sized subfield of the Conway number tree!

This milestone justifies Middle Way Mathematics adopting (No(ε₀), +, ·) as its foundational cornerstone: it provides the minimal rigorous boundary where arithmetic is guaranteed algebraic field closure without requiring a proper class.

The Ordinals

The class of ordinal numbers plays an important role in modern mathematics, modeling the concept of a collection of well-ordered elements. While most graduates of a general education model well-ordered elements solely with the natural numbers, the ordinals provide a far more comprehensive framework. Like the cornerstone axiom, the ordinals are not formally modeled from scratch in MWM; instead they are assumed as mathematical fact. In natural language, the ordinals are the equivalence classes of isomorphic (set, <w) pairs over the universe of sets and their possible well-orders, where the von Neumann ordinals provide a canonical class of representations consistent with ZF set theory.

The finite ordinals coincide with the natural numbers (as defined by finite induction). The ordinal ω is the supremum of the natural numbers, and the ordinal ε₀ is greater than ω+1.

Number Trees

MWM considers numbers to be the elements of sets defined by 2- and 4-successor inductive definitions whose stages of creation are indexed by the ordinals. As elements in the 2-successor case are indexed by paths through a binary tree, the structure is referred to as a number tree. John Conway first formulated recursive definitions for a total order among elements (nodes) of a number tree, as well as recursive definitions for addition and multiplication. It is these recursive definitions that are adopted when defining the totally ordered algebraic field (No(ε₀), +, ·).

3. Key number tree number sets

Besides the ambient field No(ε₀), MWM distinguishes two key initial segments. Letting No(α) denote the set of all number tree nodes whose birthday is strictly less than the ordinal α:

  • No(ω) is the set of nodes created on finite days, and
  • No(ω+1) is No(ω) ∪ { nodes whose birthday is ω }.

We consider the commutative ring (No(ω), +, ·) with operations inherited from No(ε₀). We demonstrate an algebraic isomorphism between (No(ω), +, ·) and (𝔻, +, ·), where 𝔻 denotes the dyadic rationals and operations on 𝔻 are those of exact dyadic arithmetic. 𝔻 serves as the arena for all numeric calculation in MWM—numeric evaluation therefore occurs within an Euclidean domain rather than an abstract field.

We do not impose a dedicated algebraic structure on No(ω+1) as a carrier set. Instead, No(ω+1) serves as a vocabulary for terms in algebraic expressions. Because its numbers reside within No(ε₀), they freely inherit field arithmetic. Notably, every standard real number embeds into No(ω+1) (x ∈ ℝ ⇒ x ∈ No(ω+1)), while No(ω+1) also accommodates non-finite numbers and infinitesimals—including the elementary step dx = 1/ω.

The 4-Successor Tree: 2D Grids and Infinitesimal Area

While 1D arithmetic and the 1D continuum are modeled along a 2-successor binary tree branching in opposing directions {+1, -1}, 2D geometry is modeled via a 4-successor tree (a quadtree) branching along orthogonal directional choices: {+1, -1, +i, -i} (or cardinal grid directions {+x, -x, +y, -y}).

  • At finite stages (up to Day ω), the 4-successor tree spans the Gaussian dyadics 𝔻[i] = { a + b·i | a, b ∈ 𝔻 }, providing exact machine arithmetic across the 2D plane.
  • At stage ω+1, orthogonal branching establishes infinitesimal resolution in both coordinate directions: an infinitesimal step dx = 1/ω along the real axis and dy = 1/ω along the imaginary axis.
  • Consequently, the 2D continuum is interpreted as an exact, discrete grid of elementary cells, each possessing an infinitesimal area:
    dA = dx · dy = (1/ω) · (1/ω) = 1/ω²

This grid interpretation provides a direct, algebraic grounding for 2D analysis, line integrals, and area integrals—bypassing the limiting processes, Lebesgue measure machinery, and continuum paradoxes of classical 2D analysis.

4. The Precision Evaluation Function

To evaluate an algebraic expression E whose number terms belong to No(ω+1), MWM defines a computable truncation map:

eval(E, p)

Given a target precision p ∈ ℕ:

  1. Each number node in E is replaced by its 2-successor tree address truncated to the first p directional choices, yielding an exact element of 𝔻.
  2. Infinitesimal terms are bounded and resolved, reproducing the standard part map (st) of nonstandard analysis.
  3. The expression resolves to an expression whose number terms are dyadic rationals.

🛡️ Lean 4 Verification Architecture: Scaffolding, Inductive Types & Proof Tiers

To ensure that the discrete computational models of Middle Way Mathematics are mathematically sound and free from circularity, the foundations are formally grounded and machine-certified in Lean 4 (see MiddleWayLean/DyadicRing.lean). This verification architecture operates with zero noncomputable axioms, establishing a constructive bridge across three parallel perspectives:

1. Triangulating the Three Models

  • The Geometric Model: Visual and spatial representations of numbers as finite paths on rooted binary trees (Tree2) and 2D quadtrees (Tree4), directly explored in the interactive engine visualizers.
  • The Executable Model: Deterministic algorithms in TypeScript/JavaScript for tree navigation, interval bisection (Conway cuts), and directed equality resolution (FSD/TTD).
  • The Certified Formal Model: Zero-axiom inductive types in Lean 4 proving ring properties, decidable order, and midpoint density via finite structural induction.

2. Inductive Base Types

  • Tree1 ≡ ℕ: Unary counting spine encoding discrete clock ticks, depth stages, and well-ordered birthdays.
  • Dyad ≡ 𝔻: Dyadic rationals m / 2^k (with m ∈ ℤ, k ∈ ℕ) forming the certified computable commutative ring (𝔻, +, ·).
  • Tree2 ≡ 𝔻: 2-successor binary trees isomorphic to the dyadic rationals via recursive left/right branch signs.
  • Tree4 ≡ 𝔻[i]: 4-successor quadtrees isomorphic to Gaussian dyadics in 2D discrete analysis.

3. Three Verification Proof Tiers

  • Tier 1 — Commutative Ring & Decidable Order: Lean-certified proofs that (𝔻, +, ·) satisfies all commutative ring axioms, possesses a decidable linear order (≤ and <), and satisfies constructive midpoint density: ∀ a < b, ∃ m, a < m ∧ m < b.
  • Tier 2 — Structural Isomorphisms: Constructive bidirectional mappings (toTree2, fromTree2) certifying that tree node paths correspond 1-to-1 with canonical dyadic values.
  • Tier 3 — Executable Pseudocode Mirrors: Lean executable definitions (such as SimplerOptions and the ConwayCut bisection walk) that mirror the TypeScript runtime line-for-line, proving termination and exact arithmetic preservation without floating-point drift.
