2. Free Fall Trajectory: Velocity & Acceleration by Pure Division

“Consider a ball thrown upward with initial speed v₀ under constant downward gravity g,” James said, writing the position polynomial on the board:

s(t) = v₀·t - (1/2)·g·t²

“On our nonstandard scaffold ℝ_ω,” James continued, “time advances by genuine hyperfinite ticks dt = 1/ω. What is the displacement over a single tick?”

s(t + dt) - s(t)  =  [ v₀·(t + dt) - (1/2)·g·(t + dt)² ]  -  [ v₀·t - (1/2)·g·t² ]
                =  [ v₀·t + v₀·dt - (1/2)·g·(t² + 2t·dt + dt²) ]  -  [ v₀·t - (1/2)·g·t² ]

s(t - dt) - 2·s(t) + s(t + dt)  =  -g · dt²

3. The Work-Energy Theorem as Telescoping Cancellation

“Now,” James asked, “where does kinetic energy (1/2)·m·v² come from, and why is mechanical energy conserved?”

Liam shook his head: “In school, we were told work is Force × distance, but no one ever proved why summing it gives (1/2)·m·v² without integral calculus.”

James walked to the center board: “Let’s look at a single time step k. Over displacement Δx_k = v_k · dt, net force is F_k = m · a_k = m · (v_{k+1} - v_k) / dt. Compute the work done on step k:”

W_k = F_k · Δx_k = [ m · (v_{k+1} - v_k) / dt ] · [ v_k · dt ] = m · v_k · (v_{k+1} - v_k)

“Notice the algebraic identity,” James pointed out:

v_k · (v_{k+1} - v_k)  =  (1/2)·[ v_{k+1}² - v_k² ]  -  (1/2)·(v_{k+1} - v_k)²

“The second term is O(dt²) hyperfinite dust! Summing over all n time steps from launch to landing:”

∑_{k=0}^{n-1} F_k · Δx_k  =  (1/2)·m·v_n²  -  (1/2)·m·v₀²  ≡  Δ(KE)
