2. The Non-Commutative Quantum Bayes Rule (Lüders Projection)

“Now,” Jack asked, “when a detector observes an outcome corresponding to projection operator P_k, how do we update the density matrix ρ?”

The Lüders Quantum Bayes Rule:
ρ' = (P_k · ρ · P_k) / Tr(ρ · P_k)

“Look at the exact 1-to-1 match with Bayes' rule from our earlier lectures!” Jack pointed out:

  • ρ is our Prior State of Knowledge.
  • P_k · ρ · P_k is the Measurement Filter (sandwiching the density matrix between projection operators).
  • Tr(ρ · P_k) = P(k) is the Evidence Denominator (the Born rule probability of witnessing clue k).
  • ρ' is our updated Posterior State of Knowledge!

Why the Order of Learning Matters (Non-Commutativity)

Jill raised her hand: “In classical Bayes, learning Clue A then Clue B gave the exact same posterior as learning Clue B then Clue A. Is that still true here?”

“Not in quantum mechanics!” Jack replied. “Because quantum projection operators do not commute (P_A P_B ≠ P_B P_A):”

P_A · P_B · ρ · P_B · P_A  ≠  P_B · P_A · ρ · P_A · P_B

“The sequence in which you interact with a quantum system physically alters the resulting reality!”

3. von Neumann Entropy: Measuring Total Ignorance

“Just as Claude Shannon measured classical uncertainty with H(P) = -∑ p_k ln p_k, John von Neumann generalized entropy to quantum density matrices:”

S(ρ) ≡ -k_B · Tr(ρ · ln ρ) = -k_B ∑_{k=0}^{ω-1} λ_k · ln(λ_k)

where λ_k are the eigenvalues of ρ.

  • For a Pure State (complete knowledge), S(ρ) = 0.
  • For a Maximally Mixed State (total ignorance), S(ρ) = k_B · ln(ω).
