Quantum Measurement as Vector Projection & The Lüders Filter

“Welcome to Lecture 3!” Jack said.

1. From Subset Slicing to Vector Projection

“In classical Bayesian inference, observing data E acts like a cookie cutter on the 1D transect ℝ_ω. It slices out the subset of points incompatible with E.”

“In quantum mechanics, states are directional arrows in complex Hilbert space ℋ_ω. Measuring a quantum state in direction |u⟩ acts as a geometric projector: it drops a perpendicular from the state vector onto the measurement axis and rescales the surviving arrow back to unit length!”

Classical Bayes: Slicing Subsets on ℝ_ω  ⇔  Quantum Bayes: Projecting Vectors on ℂ_ω

2. The Geometric Probability Law (The Dot Product)

“Suppose a photon is prepared in state vector |v⟩ (a unit arrow). If we set up a detector pointing along unit direction |u⟩, what is the probability of detection?” Jack asked.

“The Geometric Law: The probability is simply the squared cosine of the angle θ between the two vectors:”

P(Detection) = |⟨u | v⟩|² = cos²(θ)
  • Parallel / Aligned (θ = 0°): cos²(0°) = 1.00 ⇒ 100% Certainty.
  • Perpendicular / Orthogonal (θ = 90°): cos²(90°) = 0.00 ⇒ 0% Probability (Impossible).
  • Diagonal (θ = 45°): cos²(45°) = (1/√2)² = 0.50 ⇒ 50% Probability (Fifty-Fifty Split).

3. Solving the 3-Polarizer Mystery with Vector Geometry

“Now,” Jack said, “we can solve the sunglasses puzzle from Lecture 1 using pure 2D geometry!”

  1. Initial State: Light passes Filter A (Horizontal 0°). The state vector is pointing along the horizontal X-axis:
    |v₀⟩ = (1, 0)^T   (Horizontal 0°)

  2. Inserting Filter C (at 45°): The angle between 0° and 45° is θ = 45°. The probability of passing is cos²(45°) = 50%. The surviving light vector drops a perpendicular onto the 45° axis, physically rotating into:
    |v₁⟩ = (1/√2, 1/√2)^T   (Diagonal 45°)

  3. Reaching Filter B (Vertical 90°): The angle between 45° and 90° is again θ = 45°! The probability of passing is another cos²(45°) = 50%.
Total Light Output = 50% × 50% = 25%!

Jill smiled in triumph: “The diagonal filter didn't open a secret doorway — it rotated the vector into a 45° direction that had a non-zero perpendicular projection onto the vertical filter!”

4. The Quantum Bayesian Filter (The Lüders Projection Rule)

“Just as Bayes' rule updates a classical probability distribution upon learning evidence, the Lüders Projection Rule updates a quantum state upon measurement:”

Classical Bayesian Update (ℝ_ω) Quantum Bayesian Update (ℂ_ω)
Prior State:
Probability vector P(s) on state space Ω		 Prior State:
Directional state vector |ψ⟩ in Hilbert space ℋ_ω
Evidence Filter:
Slice subset indicator 𝕀_E		 Measurement Filter:
Projection operator P_E = |u⟩⟨u|
Normalization Factor (Evidence):
P(E) = ∑_{s ∈ E} P(s)		 Normalization Factor (Evidence):
P(E) = || P_E |ψ⟩ ||² = |⟨u | ψ⟩|²
Posterior State:
P(s | E) = (P(s) · 𝕀_E(s)) / P(E)		 Posterior State:
|ψ'⟩ = (P_E |ψ⟩) / √P(E)
