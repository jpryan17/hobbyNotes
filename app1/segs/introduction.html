Toward a Theoretical Maximum:
A Curriculum for General Education in General Science
— A Constructive Path from Logic to Quantum Reality —


Middle Way Mathematics is an interactive formal science textbook designed to be a robust proof of concept for a general formal science curriculum that serves the needs of the 75% or so of students not bound for STEM related careers in a manner consistent with the needs of students preparing for further STEM related study. 

Our motivation is that current theories of physical reality are among the pinnacles of human culture, and should be made as available as possible to as many as possible. Thus the curriulum focuses on the mathematics of mathematical physics.

The page is an artifact of an ongoing pair-programming dialogue between a human collaborator and an embedded machine intelligence.  As an AI agent is responsible for much of this page, the chance that it merely reflects a shared hallucination cannot be ignored; so an effort was made to minimize this possibility. First, the page uses formal statements to describe mathematical concepts. These statements are quantified predicate expressions, i.e. encoded in First Order Logic.  And all statements are verified by the Lean theorem prover. Thus the mathematical description is at least consistent. Second, calculations are defined relative to a formal statement. A calculation is described by pseudo-code, which is interpreted by a 'calculation machine' that is independent of any access to runtime resources for mathematical calculation. This dual approach of verifying the conceptual description and demonstrating its support for calculation, add weight to the claim that this page is perhaps more than a mere shared hallucination.

1. Core Educational Responsibilities & Complementary Roles

A robust curriculum in general science serves two complementary goals:

  1. General Scientific Literacy: Provide all students with a clear, conceptual, and foundational understanding of how modern science models the physical world.
  2. Prerequisite Technical Preparation: Provide future STEM practitioners the prerequisites for future studiers.

In order to simplify the description of mathematics, the curriculum leverages the Conway number tree to ground a nonstandard constructive description of numbers that accepts that the infinitesimal 1/omega (where omega is the supremum of the natural numbers) is a number. Doing so eliminates the need for limit theory. In addition, the fact that numbers are associated with tree addresses allows topology and measure to be described without introducing any additional intrinsic topological and measure spaces. The simplified mechanics allows mathematical concepts to be described more simply, and although STEM oriented students will probably need additional courses in preparation for studies in standard analysis and use of the calculation toolset based on standard analytic techniques. However, these students too will benefit from a direct, constructive path through formal science that target current theories of physical reality.

2. Epistemological Compass: Lived Phenomena vs. Mathematical Models

Throughout this curriculum, we maintain a clear and essential epistemological attitude: the rigorous distinction between physical phenomena and mathematical models.

Physical Reality & Phenomena (The Territory)

• Fixed and Objective: The physical universe exists and unfolds independently of human observers or symbolic representations.

• Direct Experience: Tangible, lived phenomena—the turning of a shadow, the solidity of a wooden desk, heat diffusing through a metal bar, or light passing through polarizers—are the primary empirical touchstones of science.

• Non-Monotonic Discovery: In the natural world, we never possess complete axioms. Empirical inquiry is always open to surprise; a single new experimental observation can revise or overturn a prevailing hypothesis. 		Mathematical Formalisms & Models (The Map)

• Human Inventions: Scientific theories—from Euclidean geometry and Newtonian kinematics to Boltzmann ensembles and Quantum Density Operators (ρ)—are formal tools crafted by human minds.

• Deductive Precision: Mathematical logic is strictly monotonic. Once a theorem is deduced from axioms, it remains permanently valid within its formal domain.

• Reasoning Under Uncertainty: Our models do not dictate how nature "must" behave; rather, they serve as rigorous, evolving lenses to organize observations, quantify ignorance, and update beliefs rationally.

But epistemological consideration does not stop there. The mathematics used to model physical theories is itself a model of a mathematical universe. For example, we do not believe numbers are addresses on a Conway number tree. But we do believe this model of number is more efficacious than its standard construction. In many ways, improvements to physical models reflect improvements to modeling mathematics. Our model of the mathematical universe is what one might call effective. It is designed to support a general education in formal science. We know the universe of numbers is not covered by our model. We make no claims as to the foundational nature of our model. But we do claim its logical consistency, and it seems to serve its purpose well.

3. Choosing the Summit: Quantum Statistical Mechanics

Our mission is to provide a mathematical foundation for understanding current theories of physical reality. Of course there are many such theories, so to sharpen the focus, we choose to formally describe Quantum Statistical Mechanics and Inference:

  • Why not the Standard Model of Particle Physics? High-energy particle interactions are fundamental, but they are detached from everyday human experience.
  • Why not General Relativity? General relativity is an elegant geometric theory, but it describes only gravity.
  • Why Quantum Statistical Mechanics? It describes the tangible everyday reality we directly interact with—how solid matter, temperature, pressure, and thermal equilibrium emerge from statistical averages over trillions of microscopic quantum degrees of freedom.

4. Curricular Levels: A Cumulative Single-Root Tree

To reach this summit with minimal cognitive load, the curriculum functions as a cohesive single-root tree organized into progressive curricular levels:

Level 1: Logic & Number

  • Propositional Logic: Grounding formal science in truth tables, Boolean connectives (¬, ∧, ∨, →, ↔), and deductive certainty.
  • Formal Statements: The language of sets, tuples, and predicates as functions into 𝔹, visualized on the 2D Boolean Relation Matrix.
  • Numbers & Graph Trees: Well-ordering on ℕ, transfinite Day ω as the supremum limit ordinal, and generating 1D ℝ_ω and 2D ℂ_ω continua with infinitesimal step size dx = 1/ω.

Level 2: Continuum & Calculus

  • Continuous Analysis & Sequences: Hyperfinite difference quotients, nonstandard microscopes, and the telescoping Fundamental Theorem of Calculus.
  • 2D Analysis & Conformal Maps: Hyperfinite 2D grids, discrete contour integrals, and phase evolution.

Level 3: Space, Direction & Geometry

  • Linear Algebra & Vector Spaces: Emergent group structures, linear transformations, and geometric duality.
  • Trigonometry & Rotor Geometry: Rotations as algebraic operators, angle measures, and geometric products.

Level 4: Growth & The Logarithm

  • The Transcendental Engine: Exponential compounding, natural logarithms, circular dynamics, and Euler's formula e^(iθ).

Level 5: Probability, Information & Quantum Logic

  • Bayesian Inference & Statistical Ensembles: Non-monotonic reasoning on state spaces (Ω) and Boltzmann statistical ensembles (Jaynes MaxEnt).
  • Quantum Logic & Measurement: Why physical polarization breaks Boolean Venn diagrams (the 3-polarizer experiment), complex amplitude arrows on ℂ_ω, and projection measurement.
  • Quantum Bayesian Inference: Density Operators (ρ), the Lüders Quantum Bayes rule, and von Neumann entropy explaining macroscopic emergence.

Level 6: Applied Seminars & Horizons

  • Physical & Mathematical Horizons: Newtonian mechanics, 1D heat diffusion, Fourier duality, cosmology, and particle zoo logic.

5. Conceptual History: The Four Epochs of Analysis

To understand why nonstandard analysis is so empowering for general scientific literacy, one must examine how mathematics historically struggled to tame continuous change:

  • Epoch 1: Intuitive Infinitesimals (17th–18th Century) — Leibniz, Newton, Euler:
    Calculus was invented using infinitesimals (dx, dy)—quantities strictly greater than zero yet smaller than any positive standard real number. Derivatives were simple algebraic ratios (dy / dx) and integrals were genuine sums of microscopic rectangles (∫ y dx).

  • Epoch 2: The Epsilon-Delta Purge (19th Century) — Cauchy, Weierstrass, Dedekind:
    19th-century mathematicians banished infinitesimals, replacing intuitive ratios with dense epsilon-delta (ε-δ) limit definitions. While watertight, this reform erected a massive cognitive barrier that alienated non-specialists from calculus.

  • Epoch 3: The Topological Escape (Early–Mid 20th Century) — Hausdorff, Lebesgue, Bourbaki:
    Mathematicians ascended into abstract point-set topology and Lebesgue measure theory. This abstraction was powerful for functional analysis, but it severely detached continuous mathematics from intuitive spatial geometry.

  • Epoch 4: The Nonstandard Synthesis — Abraham Robinson & John Conway's Number Tree:
    Infinitesimals were given complete, rigorous mathematical foundations through model theory and Conway's recursive number tree. Infinitesimals (dx = 1/ω) are legitimate numbers born on transfinite Day ω, restoring differentiation to pure algebraic division f'(x) = st(Δy / dx) and integration to discrete sums ∫ f(x) dx = st(∑ f(x) · dx).
