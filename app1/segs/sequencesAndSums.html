Sequences, Sums & Progressions

The doorway to continuous analysis: from the discrete stepping of trees to the algebra of ladders, musical scales, and telescoping sums.

Curricular Gateway: From Static Geometry to Dynamic Stepping

In our study of Geometry, we examined static spatial invariants: fixed vectors, angles, basis rotations, and metric distances. Analysis begins the moment we allow quantities to step. Before introducing rates of change or continuous motion, we examine the most fundamental discrete pattern: stepping through a sequence F(0), F(1), F(2), ..., F(n) and accumulating its differences.


Demystifying F: The Sequence as a Function-Type

In casual speech, we often speak of a "sequence" as if it were a static list of numbers. But in the formal architecture of Middle Way Math (and Lean 4), every sequence F is a FunctionType:

FunctionType = Directed Pair (The Channel) + Rule Type (The Assignment Rule)
  • The Channel (Function-Type): For sequences, the channel is ℕ_ω → ℝ_ω. The domain is the transfinite counting spine ℕ_ω = ℕ ∪ {ω}, and the codomain is the hyperreal continuum ℝ_ω. This channel guarantees that quantities advance step-by-step up to the horizon ω.
  • The Rule (The Function's Personality): What gives meat to that mysterious F? Its assignment rule k ↦ F(k) is almost always determined by a function from a specified domain:
    • From Polynomial Functions: Taking f(x) = c · x³ from the continuum function domain and evaluating it on step indices yields the cubic sequence rule F(k) = c · k³.
    • From Exponential Functions: Taking f(x) = a · r^x yields the geometric progression rule F(k) = a · r^k.
    • From Physical Energy Functions: Taking f(v) = ½ · m · v² evaluated at velocity steps v_k = k · Δv yields the telescoping work-energy ladder.

In short: the sequence function's type provides the stepping channel; its rule inherits its shape directly from a function!

2. Musical Scales: The Human Ear as a Geometric Processor

The geometric progression is not an abstract invention—it is hardwired into human sensory biology.

When we listen to music, our pitch perception is fundamentally logarithmic rather than linear. We perceive equal musical intervals (such as an octave, a fifth, or a semitone) when the physical sound frequencies form equal ratios, not equal differences.

The 12-Tone Equal Temperament Ladder

An octave is a pure frequency doubling: a ratio of 2 : 1. To divide the octave into 12 perceptually equal semitones (the 12 keys of an octave on a piano), the frequencies must advance by a constant multiplicative ratio:

r = 2^(1/12) ≈ 1.059463...

Starting from standard concert pitch A4 = 440 Hz, the chromatic musical scale is literally a discrete geometric progression:

f_k = 440 · (2^(1/12))^k   Hz   (k = 0, 1, 2, ..., 12)

After k = 12 steps, f_12 = 440 · (2^(1/12))^12 = 440 · 2 = 880 Hz, completing the octave at A5.

The Cochlear Frequency Sorter

Inside the inner ear, the basilar membrane physically separates sound frequencies along its length. High frequencies resonate near the stiff base, while low frequencies resonate near the flexible apex. Because the spatial resonant frequency drops exponentially along the membrane, equal physical distances along the human ear's sensor corresponds to equal pitch intervals—a living geometric progression analyzer.

3. The Sum of a Progression & The High School Trick

How do we sum a geometric progression? In secondary school algebra, students are introduced to the celebrated "shift-and-subtract" method:

S_n = a + a·r + a·r^2 + ... + a·r^(n-1)
r · S_n =    a·r + a·r^2 + ... + a·r^(n-1) + a·r^n

Subtracting the second equation from the first collapses every single interior power:

(1 - r) · S_n = a - a · r^n  ⇒  S_n = a · (1 - r^n) / (1 - r)

Notice what made this miraculous calculation work: all intermediate terms cancelled pairwise. Only the initial boundary term a and the outer boundary term a · r^n survived.

4. Telescoping Sums: A Nod Toward the Fundamental Theorem

The shift-and-subtract trick for geometric series is not an isolated algebraic accident. It is a specific instance of a universal telescoping identity—an algebraic cancellation pattern that will soon serve as our bridge to continuous analysis.

The Universal Telescoping Cancellation

For any sequence function F : ℕ_ω → ℝ_ω—regardless of which algebraic rule F adopts—if we define the forward step difference:

ΔF(k) = F(k + 1) - F(k)

Then the sum of differences collapses identically to the net boundary difference:

∑_{k=0}^{n-1} ΔF(k) = [ F(1) - F(0) ] + [ F(2) - F(1) ] + ... + [ F(n) - F(n-1) ] = F(n) - F(0)

Because the theorem quantifies universally over ∀ (F : ℕ_ω → ℝ_ω), it holds for every possible rule! Whether we plug in the cubic rule F(k) = c · k³, the odd-sum rule F(k) = k², or the work-energy rule F(k) = ½ m v_k², the interior terms must vanish identically.

This finite algebraic identity—which in the analysis lectures ahead will blossom into the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus (FTC)—is machine-verified in Lean 4 as MiddleWay.telescoping_ftc.

Numeric Sequences vs. Function Sequences

Here we observe an important architectural distinction that will recur throughout advanced analysis:

  • Numeric Sequence (ℕ_ω → ℝ_ω): At each step index k, the sequence outputs a single numerical scalar (a ladder rung, a partial sum, or a particle position).
  • Function Sequence (ℕ_ω → FunctionSpace ≡ ℕ_ω → (ℝ_ω → ℝ_ω)): At each step index k, the sequence outputs an entire spatial function F_k(x) holding across the full continuum domain (such as progressive Taylor polynomial curves, Fourier wave sums, or heat diffusion time slices across a spatial bar).

Both share the discrete stepping channel on ℕ_ω, but a function sequence carries an entire spatial profile forward at every step.

5. From Step Index k to Continuum Transect x

Once we recognize that summation is boundary cancellation, continuous calculus requires only one conceptual refinement:

  1. Replace the integer step index k with grid nodes x_k = a + k · dx, where dx is an infinitesimal step.
  2. The difference quotient becomes the derivative rate: f(x_k) = ΔF(k) / dx.
  3. The telescoping sum becomes the definite Riemann-hyperfinite integral:
    ∫_a^b f(x) dx = ∑_{k=0}^{N-1} f(x_k) dx = ∑_{k=0}^{N-1} ΔF(k) = F(b) - F(a)

Calculus is not an esoteric regime detached from elementary mathematics; it is the exact same pairwise boundary cancellation students learn in high school algebra, operating across an infinitesimal grid.
