By the time the subject of a formal description of numbers is presented to students, they should already be well versed in basic number literacy. The formal description will not facilitate calculation. Rather the goal is provide a formal foundation for a formal means of describing the validity of a mathematical model that bypasses the set's intrinsic spaces of continuity and measure, determined by constrained choices (referred to as a topology and a σσ-algebra) of a member of the set's powerset, the support standard analysis and measure theory. In addition, no algebraic structure is required, or possible really.

We are describing core topics. Intrinsic spaces and algebraic structures are essential for the STEM-oriented student.

We can, from our tertiary level of understanding, describe three sets of numbers defined by transfinite inductive definitions, and whose birthday is less than or equal to ωω (omega), the first non-finite ordinal. All three sets have a single root member. Their difference is determined by the number of successor functions in the definition, which is 1, 2, or 4.

When the number of successor functions is 1, only one additional member is added to the set of natural numbers, namely ωω. Defining the set
  • ℕω≡ℕ⋃{ω}ℕ_ω\equivℕ⋃\{ω\} 

isn't very interesting. But it does introduce the critical notion of introducing ωω as a member, like any other, of a set of numbers like .e.g. ℕωℕ_ω.

Things get interesting when the number of successors is 2 or 4, and the (inclusive) induction cutoff is ωω. Here, we define sets

  • ℂω≡{dyadiccomplexnumbers}⋃{(4successor)numberswhosebirthdayisω}ℂ_ω\equiv \{\mathrm{dyadic\;complex\;numbers}\}⋃ \{\mathrm{(4\;successor)\;numbers\;whose\;birthday\;is\;ω}\}  

and where ℝ⊂ℝωℝ⊂ℝ_ω and ℂ⊂ℂωℂ⊂ℂ_ω.

Without further reflection, we might think we'd leave ℝωℝ_ω and ℂωℂ_ω for STEM-oriented students, and extract ℝℝ and ℂℂ for our core number sets, but we adopt the opposite approach: ℝωℝ_ω and ℂωℂ_ω are the core number sets, and ℝℝ and ℂℂ are extracted for STEM-oriented topics such as intrinsic spaces and algebraic structure. This choice provides a framework for nonstandard analysis and measure, as we now have simple transfinite transect and grid at our disposal.

Of course, we are discussing things top down. We certainly don't want to include transfinite induction as part of the origin narrative. We haven't even formally defined union. But we note an important pattern shared by ℝωℝ_ω and ℂωℂ_ω that facilitates a simpler description:

  • there is a countable set generated by finite induction,
  • there is an uncountable set generated by induction whose birthday is ωω,
  • and the countable set is dense in their union.

We can use the recursive definitions of order and field level arithmetic pioneered by John Conway to define operations that work for finite induction and extend to work for induction step ωω.

The Power of Graphs

There is a profound geometric unity underlying this construction: balanced trees are the direct visual and topological manifestation of inductive definitions. The branching number of the graph corresponds precisely to the number of successor operations:

  • 1-Successor (The Linear Ray → ℕωℕ_ω):
    A tree with branching factor 1 is a trunk without branches—a linear ray extending toward the transfinite horizon ωω. It supports ordinal counting, but lacks the branching capacity to partition space.

  • 2-Successors (The Binary Tree → ℝωℝ_ω):
    A tree with branching factor 2 generated by signs {−,+}{ -, + } constructs the dyadic rationals and the real hyperfinite continuum. Each path defines a precise cut on the line. In probability, this 1D hyperfinite transect supports classical real-valued weights P(x)∈[0,1]P(x) ∈ [0, 1] and Bayesian belief revision.

  • 4-Successors (The Quad-Tree → ℂωℂ_ω):
    A tree with branching factor 4 generated by the four unit directions {+1,−1,+i,−i}{ +1, -1, +i, -i } constructs the Gaussian dyadics and the complex hyperfinite plane. In physics, this 2D hyperfinite grid supports complex probability amplitudes ψ(x)∈ℂωψ(x) ∈ ℂ_ω, phase rotations, and quantum wave interference.

By viewing numbers as graph trees, every number possesses an exact address (its path), an exact generation (its birthday depth), and a transparent geometric relationship with its neighbors. Graphs transform abstract transfinite set theory into concrete, visual structures.

Two Fundamental Display Perspectives: Cartesian vs. Polar Views

Beyond the choice of branching factor, graph representations of number trees possess two complementary geometric projections that illuminate completely different mathematical properties:

  • 1. The Cartesian (Hierarchical) View — Order & Continuum:
    In the standard Cartesian tree layout, the vertical axis measures birthday depth (y=dy = d), while the horizontal axis (xx) projects nodes according to their numerical order on the real line.

    • What it illuminates: Total ordering (<<), Dedekind/Conway cuts between left and right sets, and dyadic interval partitions.
    • Application: The 1D linear transect of ℝωℝ_ω, cumulative distributions, and classical real probability measures P(x)∈[0,1]P(x) ∈ [0, 1].

  • 2. The Polar (Radial) View — Phase & Rotation:
    In the radial polar layout, the root originates at the center (0,0)(0, 0). Birthday depth expands outward along concentric radial rings (r=dr = d), while successor branches diverge along angular coordinates (θθ).

    • What it illuminates: Rotational symmetry, angular phase shifts (θ↦θ+π/2θ ↦ θ + π/2), and complex unit multiplication (×i× i).
    • Application: The 2D complex plane ℂωℂ_ω, phase angles of complex probability amplitudes ψ(x)=r⋅eiθψ(x) = r * e^(iθ), and wave interference.

The Jump to Standard Analysis: Families of Sets & Intrinsic Spaces

In our core track, numbers come equipped with intrinsic geometric and algebraic coordinates through their inductive tree addresses in ℝωℝ_ω and ℂωℂ_ω. Every neighborhood, infinitesimal interval, and dyadic slice is already built into the graph.

Standard continuous analysis, however, discards these discrete tree branches and views the real numbers ℝℝ and complex numbers ℂℂ as an unstructured, uncountable dust of points. To recover continuity, limits, open neighborhoods, and area/volume integration, standard analysis cannot rely on simple pairwise unions (A⋃BA ⋃ B) or intersections (A⋂BA ⋂ B). It must glue together infinite collections of subsets simultaneously.

This motivates the concept of an indexed family of sets—a systematic way to label an entire collection of subsets using an index set II. The whole architecture of standard analysis is governed by set cardinality—the size permitted for this index set:

  • Finite Families: Allow basic Boolean combinations, but cannot capture limiting behavior.
  • Countable Families (indexed by ℕℕ): Provide the exact scope needed for measure theory and probability spaces (σ-algebras), where countably infinite sums of weights converge reliably.
  • Arbitrary (Uncountable) Families (indexed by ℝℝ or beyond): Provide the scope needed for topological spaces, allowing every single point in the continuum to contribute an open ball to a general union.

In this way, the transition from discrete graph trees to the continuous spaces of STEM topics is mediated by moving from binary set operations to indexed families of sets.
