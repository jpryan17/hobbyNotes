1. The Great Illusion: Why Does a Table Feel Solid?

“If every atom is a probabilistic wave,” Jill asked, “why doesn't the table wobble or vanish into thin air?”

“Because of the Law of Large Numbers!” Jack answered.

  • The wooden desk contains roughly N ≈ 10²⁴ atoms.
  • At the microscopic level, each atom fluctuates quantum-mechanically.
  • When you average over 10²⁴ independent quantum states, relative microscopic fluctuations shrink at the rate 1 / √N ≈ 10⁻¹² (one part in a trillion!).

“The macroscopic solidity and stability we touch every day is not the absence of quantum mechanics; it is the magnificent triumph of ensemble statistics!”
