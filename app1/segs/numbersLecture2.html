Lecture 2:
tree representations of numbers
and recursive definitions for the 2-successor (inductive definition) case

Howdy folks.

Some of you might feel that my first lecture on numbers was kind of, ah, abstract. So I'm here to make excuses about that. I'm kidding. Mostly; after all, I was describing formal definitions, which of themselves have no practical use. But I'm ready to make amends, to make things concrete. Sound good?

[mostly puzzled looks, mild agreement.]

The natural numbers have a tree representation, but a rather boring one: it is a trunk with no branches; just up and up with increasing birthday.

The 2-successor inductively defined number set can be represented by a binary tree. The 2-successor case will be the focus of our exploration of the behavior of recursive definitions.

The 4-successor inductively defined number set can be represented by two binary trees that share the same root. We leave the recursive definitions in that case for an industrious student to explore, OK?

[..]

Our representations will only include numbers whose birthday is a very small number of course, just so the tree can be displayed in detail.
