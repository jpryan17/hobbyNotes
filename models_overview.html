Part 2 — Conceptual Overview: The Three Architectural Models
— Formal & Computational, Persistence, and Verification Infrastructure —

To realize our concept presentation standards—moving seamlessly from intuitive prose and interactive visuals to machine-checked formal statements, explicit function rules, and transparent execution—Middle Way Mathematics grounds the curriculum in three interlocking architectural models:

  1. The Formal & Computational Model (Ontology & Calculation Machine): Single-sorted predicate logic, ZF set theory, base sets (𝔹, ℕ, ℝ_ω, ℂ_ω), set constructors, directed equalities (computational stencils), and transparent step-by-step pseudocode rule interpretation.
  2. The Persistence Model (Relational Schema & Curriculum Repository): Relational database tables organizing the hierarchical curriculum, formal statements, directed equations, verified presets, and interactive visual sandboxes into scalable open service hubs.
  3. The Verification & Utilities Infrastructure Model (Lean 4 & Compiler Pipeline): Build-time formal theorem verification in the Lean 4 kernel, zero-axiom computable algebra, automated static asset harvesting (genLeanCache, fsCatalog), and local interactive compiler bridges.

1. The Formal & Computational Model (Ontology & Calculation Machine)

In Middle Way Mathematics, mathematical formalism and computational execution are not separate disciplines; they are two complementary perspectives on the same underlying operational architecture:

  • Crowns the ontology with Judgment: The sovereign epistemic act (Axiom, Theorem, Definition) asserting that a formal statement evaluates to true (1 ∈ 𝔹).
  • Grounded in plain-vanilla single-sorted predicate logic: A formal statement is a machine-checkable proposition composed of quantifiers, logical operators, and predicates.
  • Relies on blue-collar ZF set theory with built-in predicates: Standard membership (∈) and static equality (=)—complemented by our primary operational innovation: built-in directed equality (:= or ⟹), the executable reduction predicate relating an LHS expression stencil to its canonical RHS target.
  • Has 4 base sets: Boolean truth values (𝔹 = {0, 1}), and 3 number sets (ℕ, ℝ_ω, ℂ_ω).
  • Has two fundamental set constructors: Cartesian product (×) and directed pair / function space (→), where predicates and functions are composed of directed pairs and executable rules.
  • A Self-Contained Calculation Machine: A directed equality pairs a verified Lean theorem with an executable rule interpreter (structured pseudocode). The pseudocode is executed by a transparent calculation machine operating strictly on the dyadic rational ring 𝔻 without relying on floating-point approximations, external continuous calculus libraries, or black-box math packages.

2. The Persistence Model (Relational Schema & Curriculum Repository)

The persistence model organizes the ecosystem across five architectural clusters:

  • Curriculum & Narrative: apps defines client targets, while curriculum_nav_items provides a recursive hierarchical topic tree linking to segments (the modular chapters) and their prerequisite progression graph (segment_prerequisites).
  • Axiomatic Verification Engine: formal_statements models mathematical theorems and definitions with self-referential parent-child hierarchies, certified directly by the Lean kernel and indexed in lean_verifications.
  • First-Class Interactive Demos: interactive_demos registers independent visual exploration sandboxes (Binary Tree Demos, Binary Interval Demos, and Truth Table Demos) that connect to both formal statements and chapter references.
  • Active Computational Stencils: directed_equations defines directional computational stencils, grounded by mode_slots (parameter ranges, symbols, units) and verified_presets (concrete JSON test cases). The relational bridge equation_statement_links explicitly connects these active formulas to the governing formal statements they instantiate.
  • Centralized Citation Hub: segment_references links inline <fs-ref>, <fsd-ref>, calculator, and demo citations in HTML segments directly to their governing formal statements, directed equations, verified presets, and interactive visual applications.

3. The Verification & Utilities Infrastructure Model (Lean 4 & Compiler Pipeline)

Lean 4 is an open-source programming language and interactive theorem prover based on the Calculus of Inductive Constructions. Lean supports formal statements that can be verified and used for calculation. MWM uses Lean as an authoritative formal verifier to certify structural consistency and commutative algebraic laws, while handling concrete numeric calculation—and its relation to formal statements—internally via the dyadic arithmetic engine.

The Middle Way Mathematics public website (middlewaymath.app) is deployed as a static web application without a live runtime dependency on Lean. Instead, a dedicated suite of Node utilities and build-time pipelines compiles, verifies, and caches proof information from the local Lean installation into production assets. Middle Way Mathematics organizes its formal verification across two complementary source files in MiddleWayLean/:

  • The Semantic Scaffold (MiddleWayLean/Scaffold.lean): Defines the primitive set-types (Tree1, Tree2, Tree4), the transfinite continuum (ℝ_ω, ℂ_ω), infinitesimal calculus operators, and the standard-part shadow map (st: ℝ_ω → 𝔻), while proving key inductive bridges like the Telescoping Fundamental Theorem of Calculus.
  • The Computable Dyadic Ring (MiddleWayLean/DyadicRing.lean): Establishes a zero-axiom computational core that machine-verifies (𝔻, +, ·) as an authentic commutative ring with decidable order, midpoint density, and executable Conway cut bisections.

The Build Pipeline and Lean Utilities

The development workflow uses a dedicated suite of Node utilities to inspect the curriculum, verify theorems against the Lean kernel, and compile static distribution assets:

  • Lean Cache Generation (npm run leanCache): Invokes lean.exe via subprocess on the dev machine to verify all theorems in Scaffold.lean. It captures the verification status, type signatures, and millisecond execution timings, storing them in static cache files (clientLib/leanCache.json and clientLib/leanCache.ts). If the scaffold file has not changed, it skips redundant re-verification.
  • Stencil Harvesting (npm run stencils): Scans the curricular HTML segments for custom <fsd-ref> tags and maps them directly to corresponding scaffold keys. It generates the catalog files (clientLib/fsCatalog.json and app1/public/fsCatalog.json), emitting formal statements (with hierarchy and types), calculation modes, and working examples.
  • Segment & Index Compilation (npm run segs, npm run index): Bundles all 58 curriculum segments and client scripts into the single static production distribution (app1/dist/index.html), embedding verified proof badges directly so readers on the public site (middlewaymath.app) require no cloud backend or Lean installation.
  • Local Interactive Compiler Bridge (npm run leanServer): An optional Express and WebSocket server running on port 3001 on the dev machine. It receives formal statements from the browser-based development application and invokes lean.exe in real time, allowing curriculum authors to edit formal lemmas and receive instant kernel diagnostics in the browser.

The Formal Verification Spectrum & Tier Levels

In modern theorem proving, mathematical formalization spans a multi-tier spectrum:

  • Tier 1 (Type Signatures & Ontology Specification): Defining vocabulary, primitive types, and operator signatures to eliminate ambiguous notation and certify well-typed formulas.
  • Tier 2 (Axiomatic Semantic Scaffold): Postulating the algebraic continuum (ℕ_ω, ℝ_ω, ℂ_ω) and standard-part shadow map (st: ℝ_ω → 𝔻) as an authoritative structural blueprint, while formally proving key inductive bridge theorems.
  • Tier 3 (Certified Code & Computable Models): Formalizing computational algorithms directly inside Lean and proving termination and exact bit-level semantic equivalence with mathematical truth (as in DyadicRing.lean with zero axioms).
  • Tier 4 (Universal Constructive Foundations): Constructing the entire transfinite continuum from scratch out of Dedekind cuts or Conway games across tens of thousands of lines of foundational library proofs (such as Mathlib).

Where MWM Sits: Middle Way Mathematics sits deliberately at Tier 2 (an Axiomatic Semantic Scaffold) for broad curriculum coverage, complemented by Tier 3 (a Zero-Axiom Computable Ring) for dyadic calculation. Rather than imposing the massive proof burden of constructing the entire continuum from set-theoretic scratch, Scaffold.lean specifies primitive set-types and algebraic rules as an authoritative blueprint, verifies foundational inductive bridges (such as the Telescoping Fundamental Theorem of Calculus), and provides certified type signatures for all formal curriculum statements.

Outline of the Lean Scaffold (Scaffold.lean)

All formal verification for Middle Way Mathematics is consolidated into Lean 4 source files in MiddleWayLean/. The primary scaffold (Scaffold.lean) spans 1,294 lines organized into five primary thematic areas:

  • 1. Primitive Set-Types & the Transfinite Continuum (Sections 0–2): Establishes inductive tree types for Boolean truth (𝔹), the unary counting spine (Tree1), the binary tree (Tree2), and the quadtree (Tree4). Introduces the transfinite ordinal horizon (Day ω), the three base sets (ℕ_ω, ℝ_ω, ℂ_ω), the dyadic rational subtype 𝔻 and discrete integer lattice ℤ, and the infinitesimal scale dx = 1/ω.
  • 2. Function Architecture & Analysis Operators (Section 3): Defines the Middle Way function model bundling Directed Pairs (α → β) and assignment Rules into FunctionType. Establishes continuum operators (deriv_op, integral_op), discrete sequence operators (delta_op, hyper_sum), and transfinite function sequence spaces (ℕ_ω → (ℝ_ω → ℝ_ω)).
  • 3. The Telescoping Calculus Theorem & 2D Complex Grid (Sections 4–7): Machine-verifies the Telescoping Fundamental Theorem of Calculus (telescoping_ftc, proved by finite induction on ℕ), the continuum FTC, the 2D complex grid ℂ_ω (ℝ_ω × ℝ_ω), the standard-part shadow map st: ℝ_ω → 𝔻, and conformal 2D cell geometry.
  • 4. Physics, Logic & Abstract Algebraic Structures (Sections 8–12): Formulates unitary norm preservation, Bayesian sequential updating and Shannon information entropy bounds, quantum logic with the Born probability rule and Lüders state vector projections, and abstract algebraic structures (Abelian groups, fields, vector spaces, and linear duals).
  • 5. Nonstandard Analysis, Trigonometry & Directed Morphisms (Sections 13–20): Formalizes infinitesimal halos (x ≈ y), the nucleus-halo decomposition, nonstandard derivative shadows (rule_D_w), the discrete intermediate value theorem (DIVT) and bisection, dyadic trigonometry with CORDIC rotor steering on ℂ_ω, turning differential operators (D_turn² = -I), exponential and logarithmic group morphisms (D(exp) = exp), and extended derivative stencils.
